## Overview of Fiduciary Duty

### What Is Fiduciary Duty

**Definition**: Legal obligation to act in client's best interest at all times

**Source**: Investment Advisers Act of 1940, Section 206

**Components**:
1. **Duty of Care** - Provide suitable advice based on client's needs
2. **Duty of Loyalty** - Avoid conflicts of interest or fully disclose

**Standard**: Higher than "suitability" standard for broker-dealers

**Applies To**:
- Registered Investment Advisers (RIAs)
- Investment Adviser Representatives (IARs)
- All client interactions and recommendations

---

## Duty of Care

### Requirements

**Provide Advice in Client's Best Interest:**
- Understand client's financial situation
- Assess risk tolerance and objectives
- Recommend suitable investments
- Monitor investments on ongoing basis

**Reasonable Investigation:**
- Research investment products thoroughly
- Understand risks and potential returns
- Compare alternatives
- Consider costs and fees
- Verify accuracy of information

**Example - Duty of Care Satisfied:**
- Client: Age 60, moderate risk tolerance, $500K portfolio
- Adviser: Conducts risk assessment, reviews goals, recommends 60/40 portfolio
- Documentation: IPS created, asset allocation explained, alternatives discussed
- Monitoring: Quarterly reviews, annual rebalancing

**Example - Duty of Care Violated:**
- Client: Conservative 70-year-old retiree
- Adviser: Recommends 100% equity allocation without explaining risks
- No documentation of suitability or risk discussion
- No monitoring or follow-up

### Ongoing Obligations

**Monitor Client Accounts:**
- Regular performance reviews
- Rebalancing when needed
- Adjust for life changes
- Update investment objectives

**Update Recommendations:**
- Market condition changes
- Client circumstances change
- New products or strategies available
- Tax law changes

## Duty of Loyalty

### Requirements

**Act in Client's Best Interest, Not Adviser's:**
- Avoid self-dealing
- No conflicts of interest without disclosure
- Prioritize client outcomes
- Seek best execution

**Prohibited Unless Disclosed:**
- Principal transactions (trading from adviser's own account)
- Agency cross transactions
- Proprietary product recommendations
- Revenue-sharing arrangements
- Soft dollar arrangements

**Example - Duty of Loyalty Satisfied:**
- Adviser recommends third-party mutual funds (no revenue-sharing)
- Discloses all compensation arrangements in Form ADV
- Uses independent custodian for client assets
- Documents best execution analysis

**Example - Duty of Loyalty Violated:**
- Adviser recommends proprietary fund without disclosure
- Receives undisclosed payments from product providers
- Trades from own account with client at unfavorable prices
- Churns accounts to generate commissions

### Principal Transactions

**What Are They**: Adviser buys from or sells to client from adviser's own account

**Requirements**:
- Written disclosure before transaction
- Client consent
- Reasonable price (at or better than market)
- No pattern of unsuitable transactions

**Best Practice**: Avoid unless absolutely necessary

## Conflicts of Interest

### Common Conflicts

**Compensation-Related:**
- Commission-based compensation (if also broker-dealer)
- 12b-1 fees from mutual funds
- Revenue-sharing from product providers
- Referral fees
- Soft dollar arrangements

**Business Relationships:**
- Proprietary products
- Affiliated entities (insurance, banking)
- Custodian relationships
- Third-party managers

**Personal Interests:**
- Personal trading (front-running)
- Outside business activities
- Family relationships with clients
- Gift and entertainment

### Disclosure Requirements

**All Material Conflicts Must Be Disclosed:**
- In Form ADV Part 2A (brochure)
- Before or at time of entering advisory relationship
- Updated annually or when material changes

**Plain English Disclosure:**
- Clear, not legalistic
- Specific, not vague generalities
- Prominent placement in brochure
- Client can understand actual conflict

**Example Disclosure (Good):**
> "Our firm receives 12b-1 fees from certain mutual funds we recommend (typically 0.25% annually). This creates a conflict because we have an incentive to recommend these funds over lower-cost alternatives that don't pay us. To mitigate this, we disclose all fees in your account statements and periodically review whether lower-cost options are available."

**Example Disclosure (Bad):**
> "We may receive compensation from third parties." (Too vague)

### Managing Conflicts

**Elimination** (Best): Remove the conflict entirely

**Example**: Only recommend no-load, no-12b-1-fee funds

**Disclosure** (Required): Fully disclose to clients

**Example**: Disclose revenue-sharing in Form ADV and client agreements

**Mitigation** (Additional): Reduce impact of conflict

**Example**: Cap total fees including revenue-sharing, periodic reviews for lower-cost options

## Best Execution

### Requirement

**Seek Best Execution for Client Transactions:**
- Not just lowest commission
- Consider: price, speed, likelihood of execution, settlement
- Document best execution process

**Applies To:**
- Securities transactions
- Mutual fund purchases
- ETF trades
- Bond transactions

### Factors to Consider

**Execution Quality:**
- Price improvement
- Market impact
- Speed of execution
- Likelihood of execution and settlement

**Not Just Commission:**
- $5 commission with poor fill price may be worse than $10 with better fill
- Consider total cost (commission + spread + market impact)

**Documentation:**
- Best execution policy in writing
- Periodic review of broker-dealers used
- Documentation of selection criteria

### Best Practices

**Regular Reviews:**
- Quarterly or annual review of execution quality
- Compare brokers and custodians
- Adjust if better execution available elsewhere

**Multiple Brokers:**
- Don't rely on single broker
- Compare quality and costs
- Document reasons for broker selection

## Disclosure Obligations

### Form ADV Part 2A (Brochure)

**Required Disclosures:**
- Types of advisory services offered
- Fees and compensation methods
- Disciplinary information
- Conflicts of interest
- Code of ethics
- Brokerage practices
- Review of accounts
- Client referrals

**Delivery:**
- Before or at time of entering advisory relationship
- Annually thereafter (or summary of material changes)
- Whenever material changes occur

### Form ADV Part 2B (Brochure Supplement)

**Individual Adviser Information:**
- Educational background
- Business experience (last 5 years)
- Disciplinary information
- Other business activities
- Additional compensation

**Delivery:**
- Before or at time of engagement with specific adviser
- Updated when material changes

### Form CRS (Client Relationship Summary)

**One-Page Summary:**
- Relationships and services
- Fees, costs, conflicts, and standard of conduct
- Whether firm and financial professionals have disciplinary history
- Where to get more information

**Delivery:**
- Before or at time of entering relationship
- When material changes occur
- Must be filed with SEC via IARD

## vs Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI)

### Comparison: Fiduciary vs Reg BI

| Aspect | Fiduciary (RIA) | Reg BI (Broker-Dealer) |
|--------|----------------|------------------------|
| **Standard** | Best interest at all times | Best interest at time of recommendation |
| **Duty** | Ongoing duty of care and loyalty | Transaction-based |
| **Conflicts** | Avoid or disclose all | Disclose and mitigate |
| **Monitoring** | Ongoing monitoring required | No ongoing duty |
| **Compensation** | Typically fee-based | Commission-based allowed |
| **Disclosure** | Form ADV Part 2 | Form CRS |

### Key Differences

**Ongoing Duty:**
- RIA: Must monitor and provide ongoing advice
- BD: Only best interest at time of recommendation

**Conflicts of Interest:**
- RIA: Higher standard, avoid conflicts or fully disclose and mitigate
- BD: Disclose and mitigate, but commission conflicts allowed

**Compensation:**
- RIA: Fiduciary duty applies regardless of compensation
- BD: Reg BI applies, but transaction-based compensation creates conflicts

## Best Practices

### For Investment Advisers

**Documentation:**
- Document all client interactions
- Record recommendations and rationale
- Maintain notes on suitability and risk discussions
- Keep records of all disclosures delivered

**Policies and Procedures:**
- Written fiduciary duty policy
- Conflicts of interest policy
- Best execution policy
- Personal trading policy (Code of Ethics)
- Annual compliance review

**Client Communications:**
- Provide clear, plain-English explanations
- Educate clients on fees, conflicts, and risks
- Deliver all required disclosures on time
- Update clients on material changes

**Compliance Program:**
- Designate Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
- Annual compliance review
- Training for all personnel
- Supervision of IARs
- Monitor for conflicts and violations

### Avoiding Common Violations

**Don't:**
- ❌ Recommend investments without understanding client's situation
- ❌ Fail to disclose material conflicts of interest
- ❌ Engage in principal transactions without disclosure and consent
- ❌ Accept undisclosed third-party compensation
- ❌ Make unsuitable recommendations
- ❌ Fail to monitor client accounts

**Do:**
- ✅ Conduct thorough suitability analysis
- ✅ Disclose all conflicts fully and clearly
- ✅ Seek best execution on all trades
- ✅ Monitor accounts regularly
- ✅ Document everything
- ✅ Update Form ADV annually

## Summary: Fiduciary Duty Quick Reference

**Duty of Care:**
- Provide suitable advice based on client's needs
- Reasonable investigation of investments
- Monitor accounts on ongoing basis
- Update recommendations as needed

**Duty of Loyalty:**
- Act in client's best interest, not adviser's
- Avoid conflicts of interest or fully disclose
- No self-dealing without disclosure and consent
- Seek best execution

**Disclosure:**
- Form ADV Part 2A (brochure) - annually
- Form ADV Part 2B (supplement) - before engagement
- Form CRS (relationship summary) - before relationship
- All material conflicts disclosed clearly

**Best Practices:**
- Document all recommendations and rationale
- Maintain written policies and procedures
- Conduct annual compliance review
- Educate clients on fees, risks, and conflicts
- Prioritize client outcomes over firm profits

**Key Message**: Fiduciary duty is the highest standard of care in law. As an investment adviser, you must always act in your client's best interest, avoid conflicts, and provide full disclosure. When in doubt, ask: "Is this in my client's best interest?" If not, don't do it.
