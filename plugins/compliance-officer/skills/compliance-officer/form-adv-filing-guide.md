# Form ADV Filing and Compliance Guide

Comprehensive guide to Form ADV preparation, filing, and maintaining accuracy.

## Form ADV Overview

**Purpose:** Primary registration and disclosure document for SEC-registered and state-registered investment advisers

**Filing System:** IARD (Investment Adviser Registration Depository)

**Parts:**
- **Part 1A:** Registration information (firm details, AUM, services, disciplinary history)
- **Part 1B:** State-specific information (for state-registered advisers)
- **Part 2A:** Brochure (narrative disclosure to clients)
- **Part 2B:** Brochure supplement (individual adviser information)

## Part 1A - Registration Information

### Filing Deadlines

**Annual Amendment:**
- Due within 90 days of fiscal year-end
- Must file even if no changes

**Other-Than-Annual Amendments:**
- Promptly when information becomes inaccurate
- Generally within 30 days of event

**Example Filing Schedule:**
```
Fiscal Year End: December 31
Annual Amendment Due: March 31

Events Requiring Prompt Amendment:
- Change in business address
- New disciplinary event
- Change in ownership/control
- Material change in services offered
```

### Key Items in Part 1A

**Item 1: Identifying Information**
- Firm name, CRD number
- Business addresses
- Website, social media

**Item 5: Information About Your Advisory Business**
- Number of employees
- Types of clients (individuals, institutions, etc.)
- Assets under management (AUM)
  - Regulatory AUM (for threshold determination)
  - Calculation: Securities portfolios for which provide continuous and regular supervisory or management services
  - Exclude: Non-securities assets, one-time financial plans, consulting-only

**AUM Calculation Example:**
```
Client A: $1M portfolio (discretionary management) → Include $1M
Client B: $500K portfolio (non-discretionary advisory) → Include $500K
Client C: Financial plan only, no ongoing management → Exclude
Client D: $2M including $1.5M securities, $500K real estate → Include $1.5M only

Total Regulatory AUM: $3M
```

**Item 7: Types of Clients**
- Individuals/high net worth
- Pension and profit-sharing plans
- Charitable organizations
- Corporations
- State/municipal entities

**Item 8: Participation or Interest in Client Transactions**
- Proprietary trading
- Investment in client transactions
- Material conflicts

**Item 9: Custody**
- Do you have custody of client funds/securities?
- Direct custody (hold assets)
- Indirect custody (fee deduction, SLOA)
- Surprise exam requirements

**Item 10: Control Persons**
- Officers, partners, directors
- 25%+ owners
- Names, titles, ownership percentages

**Item 11: Disciplinary Information**
- **CRITICAL - Most Common Deficiency**
- Must disclose all material facts
- Criminal actions
- Civil actions
- Regulatory proceedings
- SRO proceedings (FINRA, etc.)
- Investigations (if resulted in sanction)

**Disciplinary Disclosure Requirements:**
- Applies to firm AND control persons
- 10-year lookback (some items permanent)
- Include dismissed/settled matters
- Full narrative explanation required

### Common Part 1A Deficiencies

1. **Inaccurate AUM**
   - Overstating or understating assets
   - Including non-securities assets
   - Solution: Careful calculation, documentation

2. **Incomplete Disciplinary Disclosure**
   - Failing to disclose settled matters
   - Not disclosing control person discipline
   - Solution: Comprehensive background checks, ongoing monitoring

3. **Failure to Update Promptly**
   - Waiting for annual amendment when prompt amendment required
   - Solution: Calendar reminders for 30-day amendment deadline

4. **Inconsistencies with Part 2A**
   - Part 1A says one thing, Part 2A says another
   - Solution: Cross-check all information

## Part 2A - Firm Brochure

### Overview

**Purpose:** Narrative disclosure of firm's services, fees, conflicts, disciplinary history

**Delivery Requirements:**
- Before or at time of entering advisory contract
- Annually (within 120 days of fiscal year-end)
- Promptly when material changes

**Format:**
- Plain English (not legalistic)
- Clear, concise, organized
- 20-30 pages typical

### Required Items

**Item 4: Advisory Business**
- Firm history (when formed, ownership)
- Types of services offered
- Tailoring to individual needs
- Wrap fee programs (if applicable)
- AUM

**Example:**
```
[Firm Name] is an SEC-registered investment adviser founded in 2010. We are a fee-only firm owned by John Smith (60%) and Jane Doe (40%).

We provide the following services:
- Investment management (discretionary and non-discretionary)
- Financial planning (comprehensive and modular)
- Retirement plan consulting

We tailor our services to each client's individual needs, investment objectives, risk tolerance, and financial circumstances. Clients may impose restrictions on investing in certain securities or types of securities.

As of December 31, 2024, we manage approximately $150 million in assets on a discretionary basis and $25 million on a non-discretionary basis.
```

**Item 5: Fees and Compensation**
- Fee schedule (AUM-based, hourly, flat fee)
- Billing method (advance, arrears)
- Other fees client may pay (custodian fees, product fees)
- Compensation from sale of securities (if applicable)

**Fee Disclosure Example:**
```
Our investment management fees are based on a percentage of assets under management (AUM):

$0 - $1 million: 1.00%
$1 million - $3 million: 0.75%
$3 million - $5 million: 0.50%
Over $5 million: 0.40%

Fees are billed quarterly in arrears based on the average daily balance of the account. Fees will be deducted directly from your account with your authorization.

You will also pay fees to the custodian (typically $0-$50 per trade) and underlying fund expense ratios (typically 0.05%-0.50% annually for index funds we recommend).
```

**Item 6: Performance-Based Fees**
- Do you charge performance fees?
- If yes, describe structure
- SEC restrictions (accredited investors only)

**Item 7: Types of Clients**
- Individuals, high net worth, institutions
- Account minimums

**Item 8: Methods of Analysis and Investment Strategies**
- Fundamental analysis, technical analysis, cyclical
- Long-term purchases, short-term purchases, trading
- Material risks of strategies

**Example:**
```
We primarily use fundamental analysis combined with modern portfolio theory. We assess economic conditions, industry trends, and individual company financials to construct diversified portfolios aligned with each client's risk tolerance and objectives.

Our primary strategy is long-term, buy-and-hold investing using low-cost index funds and ETFs. We periodically rebalance to maintain target asset allocations.

Material Risks: All investing involves risk of loss. Market risk, interest rate risk, inflation risk, and company-specific risk can cause your account value to decline. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
```

**Item 9: Disciplinary Information**
- Same as Part 1A Item 11
- Narrative explanation
- Must be consistent with Part 1A

**Item 10: Other Financial Industry Activities and Affiliations**
- Registered representative of broker-dealer
- Futures, commodities
- Banking, insurance
- Other investment advisers
- Related persons

**Item 11: Code of Ethics, Participation in Client Transactions**
- Code of Ethics summary
- Personal trading policy
- Principal/agency cross transactions
- Material conflicts

**Code of Ethics Disclosure Example:**
```
We have adopted a Code of Ethics that sets forth standards of conduct expected of our personnel and addresses conflicts that may arise from personal trading.

Our personnel may buy or sell securities for their personal accounts that we also recommend to clients. To mitigate conflicts, we require:
- Pre-clearance of certain transactions
- Quarterly reporting of personal securities holdings and transactions
- Blackout periods around client trades in the same security
- Priority given to client orders

A copy of our Code of Ethics is available upon request.
```

**Item 12: Brokerage Practices**
- Criteria for selecting custodians
- Soft dollars (if any)
- Order aggregation
- Trade allocation
- Directed brokerage

**Best Execution Disclosure Example:**
```
We do not maintain custody of client assets. Clients select a qualified custodian (we recommend Schwab, Fidelity, or TD Ameritrade). We evaluate custodians based on:
- Execution quality and pricing
- Technology and reporting
- Customer service
- Financial strength
- Range of available investments

We do not receive research or other soft dollar benefits. We do not accept payment for order flow.

We may aggregate trades for multiple clients to improve execution and reduce costs. Trades are allocated pro-rata based on account size and investment objectives.
```

**Item 13: Review of Accounts**
- Frequency of reviews
- Who conducts reviews
- Client reporting

**Example:**
```
Investment management accounts are reviewed at least quarterly by your assigned portfolio manager. Reviews assess performance, adherence to investment policy, rebalancing needs, and changes in client circumstances.

Clients receive:
- Quarterly performance reports (within 30 days of quarter-end)
- Annual comprehensive review meeting
- Custodian statements (monthly)

Financial planning clients receive the plan deliverable upon completion and an annual update meeting if ongoing relationship.
```

**Item 14: Client Referrals and Other Compensation**
- Referral fees paid or received
- Custodian benefits
- Other economic benefits

**Example:**
```
We pay referral fees to unaffiliated third parties who introduce clients to us. Referral fees are typically 25% of the advisory fee for the first year. The referring party discloses the arrangement and you pay no additional fee. The referral arrangement creates a conflict as the referring party has a financial incentive to recommend us.

We participate in Schwab's institutional program, which provides us with technology, practice management support, and other benefits. We do not receive these benefits in exchange for client referrals to Schwab, and the benefits do not depend on the amount of assets custodied at Schwab. However, the benefits create an incentive to recommend Schwab as custodian.
```

**Item 15: Custody**
- Do you have custody?
- Surprise examination or delivery of account statements

**Item 16: Investment Discretion**
- Discretionary vs non-discretionary
- How authority granted (IMA)

**Item 17: Voting Client Securities (Proxies)**
- Do you vote proxies?
- If yes, describe policies and procedures
- If no, state that client votes and can contact issuer/custodian

**Proxy Voting Disclosure:**
```
We do not vote proxies on behalf of clients. You retain the right to vote all proxies for securities held in your account. You will receive proxy materials directly from the custodian or issuer. We are available to discuss proxy voting matters if you wish, but the voting decision remains yours.
```

**Item 18: Financial Information**
- Prepayment of fees (if 6+ months in advance and >$1,200)
- Financial condition reasonably likely to impair ability to meet commitments

**Item 19: Requirements for State-Registered Advisers**
- Principal executive officers and management
- Outside business activities
- Performance-based fees (state specifics)
- Disciplinary events
- Material relationships with issuers

### Annual Update Process

**Steps:**
1. Review current Part 2A thoroughly
2. Identify any changes in past year
3. Update affected sections
4. Create "Summary of Material Changes" page (if material changes)
5. Cross-check Part 1A for consistency
6. File Part 2A with annual amendment
7. Deliver to clients:
   - If material changes: Deliver updated brochure OR summary of changes with offer to provide brochure
   - If no material changes: Offer to deliver brochure

**Material vs Non-Material Changes:**

**Material (Must Deliver):**
- Change in fee schedule
- New conflicts of interest
- Disciplinary events
- Change in investment strategies

**Non-Material (Offer to Deliver):**
- Updated AUM figures
- Minor wording clarifications
- Administrative updates

## Part 2B - Brochure Supplement

**Purpose:** Provide information about individual advisers who provide advice to clients

**When Required:**
- Formulate investment advice for clients
- Have direct client contact

**Content:**
- Educational background
- Business experience (past 5 years)
- Disciplinary information
- Other business activities
- Additional compensation (from non-clients related to advisory services)
- Supervision

**Example Part 2B:**
```
JOHN SMITH, CFA, CFP®
Chief Investment Officer

Education:
- MBA, Finance, University of Pennsylvania, 2005
- BA, Economics, University of Michigan, 2000

Business Experience:
- [Firm Name], Chief Investment Officer, 2015-Present
- XYZ Wealth Management, Senior Portfolio Manager, 2010-2015
- ABC Advisors, Portfolio Manager, 2005-2010

Professional Designations:
- CFA (Chartered Financial Analyst), 2008
- CFP® (CERTIFIED FINANCIAL PLANNER™), 2010

Disciplinary Information: None

Other Business Activities: None

Additional Compensation: None beyond salary and bonus from [Firm Name]

Supervision: John reports to Jane Doe, CEO, who conducts quarterly reviews of client portfolios and annual performance evaluations.
```

**Delivery:**
- Before or at time of entering advisory relationship
- Annually (with Part 2A)
- When material changes occur

## Form CRS (Customer/Client Relationship Summary)

**Effective:** June 30, 2020

**Purpose:** Brief, plain-language summary comparing services, fees, conflicts, and disciplinary history

**Format:** Maximum 2 pages, prescribed format with required headers

**Key Sections:**

**1. Introduction**
- Are you a broker-dealer or investment adviser?
- What types of services do you provide?

**2. Relationships and Services**
- What investment services and advice can you provide me?
- Account monitoring, investment authority (discretionary/non-discretionary)
- Limited investment offerings
- Account minimums and requirements

**3. Fees, Costs, Conflicts, and Standard of Conduct**
- What fees will I pay?
- How might your conflicts of interest affect me?
- How else does your firm make money?
- What is your standard of conduct?

**4. Disciplinary History**
- Do you or your financial professionals have legal or disciplinary history?
- Link to Investor.gov free/simple search

**5. Additional Information**
- How to find more info, file complaint
- Contact information

**Conversation Starters (Required):**
- "Given my financial situation, should I choose an investment advisory service? Why or why not?"
- "How will you choose investments to recommend to me?"
- "What is your relevant experience, including your licenses, education, and other qualifications? What do these qualifications mean?"

**Delivery:**
- Before or at time of entering advisory relationship
- Posted on public website
- File with SEC (on Form CRS)

**Updating:**
- Promptly when material changes
- At least annually review for continued accuracy

## Common Compliance Deficiencies from SEC Exams

### Form ADV Deficiencies

1. **Inaccurate AUM Reporting**
   - Including non-discretionary AUM incorrectly
   - Solution: Implement controls, reconcile quarterly

2. **Incomplete Disciplinary Disclosure**
   - Missing criminal, civil, or regulatory events
   - Solution: Comprehensive background checks, ongoing monitoring

3. **Failure to Deliver Part 2A Annually**
   - Not delivering brochure to clients each year
   - Solution: Calendar reminder, track delivery

4. **Part 2A Not Matching Operations**
   - Brochure says one thing, firm does another
   - Solution: Review Part 2A during annual compliance review, update as practices change

5. **Custody Reporting Errors**
   - Incorrectly answering custody questions
   - Solution: Understand custody definition, consult custody rule

### Remediation Process

**When SEC Identifies Deficiency:**

1. **Acknowledge and Investigate**
   - Understand the issue
   - Determine root cause
   - Assess scope (how widespread?)

2. **Remediate**
   - Correct the immediate issue (update Form ADV, deliver amended brochure, etc.)
   - Implement controls to prevent recurrence

3. **Document**
   - Memorandum explaining issue, remediation, and controls implemented
   - Retain in compliance files

4. **Respond to SEC**
   - Provide written response within deadline (typically 30 days)
   - Explain remediation and controls

**Example Response:**
```
Deficiency: Part 2A Item 5 (Fees) did not accurately describe negotiability of fees

Remediation:
- Amended Part 2A Item 5 to state "Fees are negotiable"
- Delivered amended brochure to all clients
- Implemented quarterly review of Part 2A by CCO to ensure accuracy

Controls:
- Annual review checklist now includes specific verification that fee schedule matches actual practices
- New client agreements reviewed by compliance before execution
- Training provided to all advisers on fee negotiation disclosure requirements

Supporting Documentation:
- Amended Part 2A (Exhibit A)
- Client delivery log (Exhibit B)
- Compliance review checklist (Exhibit C)
- Training materials and attendance (Exhibit D)
```

## Form ADV Compliance Calendar

**Annual Tasks:**
- **Within 90 days of fiscal year-end:** File annual amendment (Part 1A and Part 2A)
- **Within 120 days of fiscal year-end:** Deliver Part 2A/2B to clients (or summary of material changes)
- **Annually:** Review Form ADV for accuracy (part of annual compliance review)

**Ongoing Tasks:**
- **Within 30 days of change:** File other-than-annual amendment for material changes
- **Promptly:** Deliver amended Part 2A to clients when material changes
- **As needed:** Update Part 2B when personnel changes or disciplinary events
- **Immediately upon learning:** Investigate and disclose disciplinary events (Item 11/Item 9)

**Best Practice:**
- Assign CCO responsibility for Form ADV
- Calendar reminders 60, 30, and 7 days before deadlines
- Quarterly reviews of Part 1A for accuracy
- Annual full review of Part 2A (even if no changes)
- Track all client deliveries (brochure, amendments)
